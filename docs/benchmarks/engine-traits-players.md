1. Perceptual and cognitive traits

These are powerful because they change decision timing, not just decision quality.

Early scanner
Checks surroundings before receiving. Improves first-time actions under pressure.
Usually mixed, but can be developed.

Blind-side awareness
Detects runners or pressure outside direct vision more often than normal.
Mostly innate.

Tempo manipulator
Naturally slows or accelerates play to destabilize shape.
Mixed.

Delayed passer
Waits an extra beat to open a better lane instead of releasing early.
Learnable.

Risk calibrator
Exceptional at choosing when to attempt high-value actions.
Learnable.

Space magnet
Finds free pockets unusually well without obvious triggers.
Mostly innate.

Press baiter
Invites pressure deliberately to free teammates.
Learnable.

Transition anticipator
Starts moving for the next phase before possession has clearly turned.
Mixed.

2. Technical micro-traits

These are not broad attributes like passing or dribbling. They are specific execution quirks.

One-touch specialist
Prefers and executes one-touch combinations at unusual efficiency.
Learnable.

Toe-poke finisher
Uses unconventional quick-release finishes in crowded areas.
Mostly innate.

Outside-foot passer
Uses outside of the boot for angle manipulation.
Learnable.

Disguised first touch
First touch is used to deceive, not just control.
Mixed.

Bounce technician
Unusually good at dealing with awkward bounces or loose balls.
Mostly innate.

Aerial redirection
Redirects headers with intent rather than just winning them.
Learnable.

Half-volley comfort
Will attempt difficult strikes others avoid.
Mixed.

Recovery touch
Recovers from poor control unusually well without losing the duel.
Mostly innate.


3. Movement and off-ball traits

These are among the best simulation traits because they produce visible identity.

Late box arriver
Times delayed runs into the box unusually well.
Learnable.

Near-post hunter
Constantly attacks near-post spaces in crossing situations.
Learnable.

Blind-side runner
Times runs on the defender’s shoulder better than normal.
Mostly innate.

Decoy mover
Makes convincing sacrificial runs that open space for others.
Learnable.

Second-ball predator
Positions for knockdowns and loose clearances unusually well.
Mixed.


Static lure
Stays still longer than expected to disconnect markers before sudden movement.
Rare, mixed.

Channel drifter
Naturally migrates into half-spaces or channels without explicit instruction.
Mixed.

Far-post ghost
Appears at the back post untracked at unusual frequency.
Learnable.

Rebound instinct
Attacks rebound zones immediately after shots more often than average.
Mostly innate.

4. Defensive and duel traits

These are useful because defenders often become flat in simulations.

Containment specialist
Prefers delaying and guiding over immediate tackling.
Learnable.

Recovery sprinter
Keeps composure and line choice during recovery runs instead of just sprinting blindly.
Mixed.

Passing-lane thief
Steals interceptions by reading the next pass unusually early.
Mostly innate.

Body-angle manipulator
Shows attackers into dead zones with unusual consistency.
Learnable.

Tactical fouler
Stops transitions cynically but intelligently.
Learnable.

Aerial grappler
Uses legal-contact manipulation before aerial duels.
Learnable.

Second-contact winner
Loses first duel but recovers the loose outcome at high rates.
Mixed.


5. Psychological and competitive traits

These should be hidden or semi-hidden. They create variance and identity.

Big-match riser
Performance lifts under high-stakes conditions.
Mostly innate.

Big-match shrinker
Drops in initiative or execution under pressure.
Mostly innate.

Momentum player
Confidence swings sharply after a good or bad action.
Mostly innate.

Error immunity
Recovers mentally from mistakes unusually fast.
Mixed.

Crowd-reactive
Boosted or destabilized by crowd hostility or support.
Mostly innate.

Provocable
More likely to lose discipline when targeted.
Mostly innate.

Referee manipulator
Adjusts behavior to referee strictness better than average.
Learnable.

Pain masker
Sustains output despite knocks better than expected.
Mostly innate.

Status sensitive
Performs better with leadership responsibility or worse when challenged.
Mixed.

Clutch executor
Late-game composure rises instead of falls.
Mostly innate.

6. Dark arts and rare matchcraft traits

These make players memorable.

Contact seller
Wins fouls by exaggerating contact convincingly.
Learnable.

Shield addict
Uses body shielding in places where others would release the ball.
Learnable.

Line-step trapper
Manipulates offside timing aggressively.
Learnable.

Quick restart opportunist
Takes restarts before shape resets.
Learnable.

Time-killer
Protects the ball or slows play intelligently late in matches.
Learnable.

Chaos creator
Prefers actions that destabilize shape even at some loss of clean possession.
Mostly innate.

Nutmeg opportunist
Attempts humiliation-based evasion in tight duels.
Mostly innate.

Bounce-room dribbler
Thrives in ugly, ricochet-heavy dribbles rather than clean carries.
Rare, innate.

Keeper disruptor
Elite at screening, bumping zones, and affecting keeper claims legally.
Learnable.

Learnable.

Cross poker

Learnable.

8. Best classification for your engine

Do not store every trait as just present/absent. Use a three-part model:

Origin

innate
learnable
mixed

Strength
Each trait effectiveness should be affected by specific attributes that logically make sense.


Expression condition

always
under pressure
in transition
final third
defensive third
late game
when fatigued
when trailing
when leading

That prevents traits from feeling like global buffs.

Example:

Penalty reader:
Origin: mixed
Expression: final third, under pressure
